Case study 01

Saudi Arabia (KSA) · Gulf Financial UX

DigitalCar: "Can I afford this?" answered before the first scroll.

Role

End-to-End Product Designer

Product

Buy, sell & finance cars under one account

Scope

61 mobile web screens · Arabic RTL system · Desktop specs

Timeline

2 months discovery · 7 days system · 10 days hi-fi

01 — The insight

Flipping the mental model: From "Pick a car" to "Discover your purchasing power."

Car platforms assume buyers start with a model. Our research showed affordability comes first, yet the old product hid financing behind a long registration form.

Before

Pick a car→Long registration form→Find out if approved

After

Discover your budget→Cars that fit it→Confirm the request

02 — Process

Research before pixels. Structure before colour.

Two months on the live product: UX audit, Hotjar recordings and heatmaps, then strategy, personas and journeys. Flows and sitemap were tested before any low-fi.

03 — Key decisions

01

Lead with the monthly payment, not the price.

Monthly installments sit on every car card, so buyers see what they can afford from the first scroll. It matches how they think: cash flow, not total price.

Key decisions · 02 of 03

02

Financing, right where the decision happens.

'Check Financing' is the main action on every car page. One calculator (salary, obligations, down payment, tenure) returns real installments and matching cars, so no one meets a false approval later.

Key decisions · 03 of 03

03

A comparison that picks a winner.

Each row highlights the winner and turns specs into plain pros and cons, with an AI co-pilot for the last doubt. Buyers move from 'Which is better?' to 'This one is mine.'

Case study 02

United Arab Emirates

Rofoof: One grocery platform for retail & wholesale.

Role

Lead Product Designer · UX/UI & Brand

Product

On-demand grocery for retail & wholesale

Scope

Consumer app · Courier app · Admin ERP · Web

Timeline

1 month research · 20 days design & handoff

01 — The insight

A household orders 500g of grapes for today. A retailer orders 10 cartons for next Thursday.

Same inventory, very different rules: units, pricing tiers, minimum orders and delivery windows. Instead of two apps, one engine adapts to who is buying.

Challenge 1 · Dynamic quantity & tiered pricing

Grams for households, cartons with minimum orders for retailers, in one catalogue.

Challenge 2 · Fulfillment & scheduling disparity

Under-60-minute delivery and multi-day wholesale scheduling, in one checkout.

02 — Process

Four products, one ecosystem.

Role-based journeys for four personas (Consumer, Courier, Branch Manager, Super Admin), from wireframes to one Light/Dark design system.

03 — Key decisions

01

One product page, two ways to buy.

One product page adapts to the buyer chosen at onboarding: per-kg prices for households, tiered bulk pricing with minimum orders for wholesale. One UI to learn, one to maintain.

Key decisions · 02 of 03

02

Instant by default, scheduled when needed.

Checkout defaults to instant delivery. One tap opens time slots or multi-day scheduling for bulk orders, so B2C stays fast and B2B stays precise in the same funnel.

Key decisions · 03 of 03

03

Every alert arrives with its fix.

Every alert and order row carries its own action, so admins assign couriers, fix delays or reorder in one click, without leaving the screen.

Case study 03

Morocco

Tourstica: Travel booking that starts with a story.

Travel booking reimagined as a cultural journey: stories and local people first, the booking second.

Role

Lead Product Designer · UX/UI & Brand

Product

Marketplace for local travel experiences

Scope

Responsive web · Storytelling modules · Identity

Timeline

2-Week Intensive Sprint

A destination first — a booking second.

01 — The insight

Destinations aren't selected from price grids. They're chosen through emotional resonance—and validated by trust.

Travel sites open with search bars and price tables. First-time visitors need desire and trust in the host first, so Tourstica leads with local stories, then asks for the booking.

02 — Process

Structure the story before the pixels.

The journey mapped as chapters: Discovery → Experiences → Hosts → Trust → Stories → Planning. Low-fi validated the order and booking touchpoints before any imagery.

Week 1 · UX

UX Strategy · Narrative Architecture, Trust Model Definition & Low-Fi Wireframing

Week 2 · Design

Visual UI System · High-Fidelity Responsive Design (Desktop & Mobile) & Asset Orchestration

✦ Full responsive design in a 2-week sprint, with AI speeding up layout and content iteration.

Low-fidelity wireframes · structure before imagery

03 — Key decisions

01

Destination first, transaction second.

The page opens with emotion, not utility. Interest-based discovery comes before any booking option, so intent is built before price appears.

Key decisions · 02 of 03

02

Trust, visible at every step.

Every experience shows a vetted host and a 4-point check (ID, licensed guide, local resident, top rated), right where safety doubts appear.

Key decisions · 03 of 03

03

No account until you are ready.

No account or payment until the host confirms. Traveller stories double as entry points, with 'Book this trip' one click away.

04 — Screens

One landing page, told in chapters.

One page that moves from inspiration to hosts, proof and an easy booking.

01 · Hero & Emotional Hook

Inspiring exploration, introducing brand ethos, and interest taxonomy.

02 · Experience Curation & Local Hosts

Highlighting authentic local itineraries and humanized host profiles.

03 · Trust Framework & Social Proof

Verification metrics, safety standards, and real post-trip traveler stories.

04 · Process Transparency & FAQ Footer

4-step frictionless booking guide, trip FAQs, and footers.

05 — Design system

Deep green grounds it. Terracotta means act.

Deep forest green sets the cultural tone. Terracotta is reserved for actions only, so users learn that terracotta means tap.

Custom wordmark with a terracotta accent, the same colour as every CTA.
